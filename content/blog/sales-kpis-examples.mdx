Sales KPIs are the handful of numbers a sales team watches to know whether it is on course to hit its targets. Good sales KPIs tell you what to fix this week, such as slow replies, missed follow-ups or quotes that never close, before the month ends and the revenue is already lost.

This guide explains the meaning of KPI in sales, gives you 20 sales KPI examples with simple formulas, shows how to pick the few that fit your business and how to review them each week. It also covers how to track sales KPIs in a CRM instead of a spreadsheet.

<KeyTakeaway title="Sales KPIs in short">

- **A KPI is a number linked to a goal.** It needs an owner and a target, or it is just a metric.
- **Track five to seven KPIs,** not twenty. Choose from lead handling, activity, pipeline, revenue and customer groups.
- **Mix leading and lagging KPIs.** Leading ones, like on-time follow-ups, let you act now. Lagging ones, like revenue, confirm the result.
- **Set targets from your own past data,** not from someone else's benchmark.
- **Review weekly for 15 minutes** and change one thing at a time.

</KeyTakeaway>

## What Are Sales KPIs? (KPI Meaning in Sales)

KPI stands for key performance indicator. In sales, a KPI is a measurable number that shows how well you are doing against a specific goal. "We want more revenue" is a goal. "Contact 90% of new leads within one hour" is a goal you can measure, and the percentage you hit is the KPI.

KPIs and metrics are not the same thing. A metric is any number you can count, such as calls made today. A KPI is a metric you picked on purpose because it moves a result you care about, and it has a target and an owner.

| | Metric | KPI |
|---|---|---|
| Linked to a goal? | Not necessarily | Always |
| Has a target? | Usually not | Yes |
| Has an owner? | Rarely | Yes |
| Example | Number of calls made | Follow-ups completed on time (target: 95%) |

### Leading vs lagging KPIs

| Type | What it shows | Examples | Use it to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading | What is likely to happen | Lead response time, on-time follow-ups, meetings booked | Change behaviour this week |
| Lagging | What already happened | Revenue won, win rate, sales cycle length | Confirm whether the plan worked |

If you track only lagging KPIs, you find out about a problem after the money is gone. Pair each result with one or two leading numbers that drive it.

## 20 Sales KPIs Every Team Should Know

Here are 20 sales KPI examples in five groups, each with a formula. You do not need all of them. Use the tables to shortlist the ones that match your sales process.

### Lead handling KPIs

| # | KPI | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New leads | Count of leads created in the period | Whether marketing is feeding the team |
| 2 | Lead response time | Time from lead created to first contact (average or median) | How fast you reply |
| 3 | Lead-to-contact rate | Leads contacted ÷ new leads × 100 | Whether leads are being reached at all |
| 4 | Conversion rate by lead source | Won deals from a source ÷ leads from that source × 100 | Which channels bring buyers, not just enquiries |
| 5 | Cost per lead | Marketing spend ÷ new leads | What each enquiry costs you |

### Activity KPIs

| # | KPI | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Calls and chats per rep | Calls and chats logged ÷ working days | Effort, per person |
| 7 | On-time follow-ups | Follow-ups done by the due date ÷ follow-ups due × 100 | Discipline in follow-up |
| 8 | Meeting show-up rate | Meetings, demos or site visits held ÷ booked × 100 | Quality of the booking and confirmation process |

### Pipeline KPIs

| # | KPI | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Qualified leads | Leads that pass your qualification rule ÷ leads contacted × 100 | Lead quality |
| 10 | Pipeline value | Sum of the value of all open deals | How much could close |
| 11 | Weighted pipeline | Sum of (deal value × stage win probability) | A more realistic forecast |
| 12 | Pipeline coverage | Pipeline value ÷ revenue target | Whether you have enough deals to hit target |
| 13 | Stage conversion rate | Deals reaching the next stage ÷ deals in this stage × 100 | Where the funnel leaks |
| 14 | Idle deals | Count of open deals with no activity for X days | Deals going cold |

### Revenue KPIs

| # | KPI | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | Win rate | Deals won ÷ (deals won + deals lost) × 100 | How well you close qualified deals |
| 16 | Average deal size | Revenue won ÷ deals won | Typical order value |
| 17 | Revenue against target | Revenue won ÷ target × 100 | Whether the month is on track |
| 18 | Sales cycle length | Average days from lead created to deal won | How long selling takes |

### Customer KPIs

| # | KPI | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | Renewal or repeat rate | Customers who renewed or reordered ÷ customers due × 100 | Whether customers stay |
| 20 | Customer acquisition cost | Sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers won | What it costs to win one customer |

For the funnel stages behind KPIs 9, 13 and 14, see our guide to [sales funnel management](/blog/sales-funnel-management).

## Sales KPIs Worked Example

Here is a month of sample numbers for a small team, to show how the formulas fit together. These are illustrative figures, not benchmarks.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| New leads | 400 |
| Leads contacted | 320 |
| Marketing spend | ₹60,000 |
| Deals won | 9 |
| Deals lost | 15 |
| Revenue won | ₹4,05,000 |
| Monthly target | ₹5,00,000 |

| KPI | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Lead-to-contact rate | 320 ÷ 400 × 100 | 80% |
| Cost per lead | ₹60,000 ÷ 400 | ₹150 |
| Win rate | 9 ÷ (9 + 15) × 100 | 37.5% |
| Average deal size | ₹4,05,000 ÷ 9 | ₹45,000 |
| Revenue against target | ₹4,05,000 ÷ ₹5,00,000 × 100 | 81% |
| Marketing cost per won deal | ₹60,000 ÷ 9 | about ₹6,667 |

What do these KPIs say? One in five leads was never contacted, and the win rate gives you a baseline to improve. The cheapest way to reach the target is probably to contact the missing 80 leads, not to buy more of them.

### Weighted pipeline in one example

Suppose you have three open deals. Weighted pipeline multiplies each deal by the win probability of its stage.

| Deal | Value | Stage probability | Weighted value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deal A | ₹2,00,000 | 20% | ₹40,000 |
| Deal B | ₹1,00,000 | 50% | ₹50,000 |
| Deal C | ₹50,000 | 80% | ₹40,000 |
| Total | ₹3,50,000 | | ₹1,30,000 |

The open pipeline looks like ₹3.5 lakh, but a realistic forecast is closer to ₹1.3 lakh. Set stage probabilities from your own history of how many deals in each stage actually close.

### How much pipeline coverage do you need?

Pipeline coverage depends on your win rate. A simple way to work it out is: coverage needed = 1 ÷ win rate. If your win rate is 25%, you need open pipeline of about four times your target. If it is 50%, you need about two times. Many teams guess a multiple; your own win rate gives a better one.

## How to Choose Your Sales KPIs

Start from the question "what is stopping us from selling more?" and pick the KPIs that measure that problem.

1. **Pick one KPI for lead handling.** For most teams this is lead-to-contact rate or lead response time.
2. **Pick one or two for pipeline health.** Stage conversion rate and idle deals show where deals leak or stall.
3. **Pick two or three for results.** Win rate, average deal size and revenue against target cover the outcome.
4. **Add one customer KPI** if repeat business matters, such as renewal rate.
5. **Give each KPI an owner and a target.** Set the target from your last three months, then raise it gradually.

### KPIs by role

| Role | KPIs to watch |
|---|---|
| Business owner | Revenue against target, win rate, conversion rate by lead source, customer acquisition cost |
| Sales manager | Pipeline coverage, stage conversion rate, idle deals, sales cycle length |
| Sales rep | Lead response time, on-time follow-ups, meetings booked and held, own win rate |

## A 15-Minute Weekly Review for Sales KPIs

Use the same simple routine every week, on the same day.

1. **Look at lead handling (3 minutes).** Lead-to-contact rate and response time. Any untouched leads? Assign them today.
2. **Look at the pipeline (5 minutes).** Idle deals, stage conversion and pipeline against target. Pick the three biggest deals and agree the next step for each.
3. **Look at results (4 minutes).** Revenue against target, win rate and the lost reasons recorded this week.
4. **Pick one change (3 minutes).** One fix, one owner, one date. Check it again next week.

## Common Sales KPI Mistakes

- **Tracking too many.** Twenty KPIs mean nobody acts on any of them. Start with five to seven.
- **Only lagging numbers.** Revenue tells you about last month. Add one or two leading KPIs that you can change this week.
- **No targets or owners.** A number without a target is a metric. A number without an owner is nobody's job.
- **Copying someone else's benchmarks.** Your price, lead quality and sales cycle are different. Use your own history.
- **Measuring activity instead of results.** More calls do not help if the leads were never qualified. Pair activity KPIs with conversion KPIs.
- **Poor data.** If stages and lost reasons are filled in carelessly, the KPIs mislead. Agree what each stage means.
- **Using KPIs to blame people.** Use them to find which stage needs help.
