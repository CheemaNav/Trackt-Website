You switched to the WhatsApp Business API, ran your first broadcast, and the bill was higher than the "₹0.80 per message" someone quoted you. Then in October 2026 your replies to customers started costing money too.

You are not imagining it. WhatsApp Business API pricing in India changed twice in two years, and most guides online still describe the old rules. Here is what you actually pay today, in rupees, with real examples for small businesses.

## WhatsApp Business API pricing in India (quick answer)

Meta charges per delivered message, by message type. For messages to Indian (+91) numbers:

| Message type | What it is for | Meta rate per message | With 18% GST |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Offers, launches, festive broadcasts, re-engagement | ₹0.8631 | ₹1.0185 |
| Utility | Appointment and site visit reminders, payment and order updates | ₹0.1150 | ₹0.1357 |
| Authentication | OTPs and login codes | ₹0.1150 | ₹0.1357 |
| Service | Your free-form replies after a customer messages you | First 1,000 per number per month free, then ₹0.1150 | ₹0.1357 after the free 1,000 |
| Authentication-international | OTPs sent to Indian users by eligible businesses based outside India | ₹2.4971 | ₹2.9466 |

Your provider (the platform or CRM you use to send messages) may add its own monthly fee or a per-message markup on top. That is the part most people forget.

## What changed in WhatsApp Business API pricing in India, and when

- **1 July 2025: per-message billing.** Meta stopped charging per 24-hour conversation and started charging for every delivered template message. Any guide that talks about "conversation pricing" is out of date.
- **1 January 2026: rupee billing and a price rise.** Meta started billing eligible Indian businesses in INR, and the marketing rate for India went up about 10%, from ₹0.7846 to ₹0.8631.
- **1 October 2026: replies are no longer fully free.** Free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer service window (service messages) now cost the utility rate after the first 1,000 per business phone number each month. Utility templates sent inside that window are also charged.

## The 3 parts of your real WhatsApp bill

1. **Meta's message charges.** The rates in the table above, multiplied by how many messages of each type you send.
2. **Your provider's fee.** A monthly platform fee, a per-message markup, or both. A markup looks tiny per message but adds up fast on broadcasts.
3. **GST at 18%.** Charged on Meta's fees and on your provider's fees.

**Formula:** (marketing messages × ₹0.8631) + (utility and authentication messages × ₹0.115) + (service messages above 1,000 × ₹0.115) + provider fees, then add 18% GST.

## WhatsApp Business App vs WhatsApp Business API

| | WhatsApp Business App | WhatsApp Business API |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free | Pay per message + provider fee |
| Who can reply | One phone, a few linked devices | Your whole team, from a shared inbox |
| Automation | Basic quick replies and away messages | Auto-replies, reminders, workflows |
| Bulk messages | Limited broadcast lists | Approved templates to opted-in customers |
| Connect to a CRM | Not officially | Yes |
| Green tick / verified business | No | Possible after Meta approval |
| Best for | Solo owner, few chats a day | Teams handling many leads a day |

If you get a few enquiries a day and reply yourself, the free app is enough. Once two or more people reply to leads, or you send reminders at scale, the API pays for itself in saved leads.

## 7 ways to cut your WhatsApp API bill

1. **Send fewer, better broadcasts.** Send offers only to segments that asked about that project, course or service. A smaller, relevant list costs less and gets fewer blocks.
2. **Use utility templates for real updates.** Reminders and confirmations cost ₹0.115, not ₹0.8631. Meta can re-categorise a "utility" template that contains an offer, so keep promotions out of them.
3. **Reply inside the 24-hour window.** When a customer messages you, reply then with a normal message instead of sending a paid template later.
4. **Track your 1,000 free service replies** per number each month, so you know when charges start.
5. **Ask providers about markups.** Compare total cost at your real volume, not the advertised monthly fee.
6. **Clean your list.** Remove numbers that never reply or have blocked you. Delivered messages cost money; undelivered ones do not, but a poor quality rating can limit how many you can send.
7. **Stop duplicate replies.** When three people reply to the same lead from separate phones, you pay three times and confuse the customer. A [shared inbox](/features/whatsapp-crm) fixes this.

One more saving to know about: when a customer starts the chat from a click-to-WhatsApp ad or the WhatsApp button on your Facebook Page, Meta opens a free entry point window, and your messages in that window are not charged.
